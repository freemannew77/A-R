Every situation is different, and laws vary by jurisdiction. These articles provide general information only, not legal advice about your circumstances.

BUSINESS · 6 MIN READ01

Planning for the moments your business can’t predict

A useful business plan also considers what happens when the unexpected arrives: a key owner leaves, a dispute disrupts operations, or an opportunity requires quick action. Start by identifying who can make decisions, where essential records are kept, and which agreements govern ownership and continuity.

Review those arrangements regularly. Clear operating documents, current authority records, and a plan for temporary leadership can reduce confusion when time matters. A lawyer and financial adviser can help adapt the plan to your business structure and goals.

ESTATE PLANNING · 4 MIN READ02

A thoughtful first step toward your family’s future

Estate planning is more than deciding who receives property. It can name trusted people to make financial or health care decisions if you cannot, provide guidance for loved ones, and reduce uncertainty about your wishes.

Begin with a current inventory of assets, existing documents, and the people you may ask to serve. Consider how life changes such as marriage, a new child, relocation, or a business sale affect the plan. Requirements differ by location, so have documents reviewed for the jurisdiction where they will be used.

REAL ESTATE · 5 MIN READ03

What to know before signing on the dotted line

Before signing a property agreement, clarify the scope of the deal, key dates, conditions to closing, responsibility for costs, and what happens if either party cannot perform. For a lease, pay special attention to renewal terms, permitted use, maintenance duties, insurance, and assignment rights.

Ask for time to review attachments and referenced documents. A careful review can surface obligations that are easy to miss in a short summary. The right questions depend on the property, the agreement, and local law.
